## 4. Evidence chain used for provenance analysis

When evaluating a disputed artifact, COSMOS uses this sequence:

**artifact → date → authorship → access/communication → technical comparison → applicable right**

### Artifact
What exact file, code block, document, diagram, dataset, model asset, design, or other material is being compared?

### Date
What reliable record shows when each artifact existed?

### Authorship
What evidence supports who created or owned the particular material?

### Access / communication
What evidence shows whether the other person received, viewed, accessed, or was exposed to the material?

### Technical comparison
What specific similarities exist in protectable expression, implementation, structure, documentation, data, or other legally relevant material?

### Applicable right
Which right, if any, actually applies: copyright, contract, trade secret, patent, trademark, confidentiality, license terms, or another legal doctrine?

Similarity alone is not the entire analysis.

## 5. Preservation protocol

For evidence that may become legally relevant, preserve the original whenever possible.

Recommended preserved fields include:

- original file name and path;
- source repository or device;
- creation/modification metadata where available;
- commit SHA or release identifier;
- cryptographic hash;
- capture date and time;
- original URL or platform reference;
- screenshot plus surrounding context;
- export format;
- identity of the person who captured/exported it;
- notes explaining what the artifact is intended to establish.

Do not alter an original merely to make it easier to present. Work from copies and preserve the source artifact.
