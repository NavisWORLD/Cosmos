# The short version

COSMOS grew from Cory Shane Davis’s long-running effort to explore computational continuity: persistent memory, dynamic internal state, association learning, sensory context, recurrent world state, evidence, and adaptive loops that continue across interactions instead of conceptually resetting at every prompt.

The research lineage Cory uses is:

**2018 → 2024 → 2025 → 2026**

- **2018:** conceptual and personal origin of the research direction.
- **2024:** formal CST research phase and the 12D framework.
- **2025:** increasingly executable memory, world-state, learning, and autonomous-study systems, together with a written collaboration record that expressly documented Cory’s claimed foundational ownership boundary.
- **2026:** integration into COSMOS as a local-first runtime/platform with reproducible engineering and provenance controls.

The present repository is the source-first engineering implementation of that lineage.

# What timestamps can and cannot prove

Repository commits, archived files, DOI records, releases, and signed/hashed evidence can help establish chronology.

They can support statements such as:

> “This specific artifact existed by this recorded date.”

They do **not automatically prove**:

- that another person saw the artifact;
- that another implementation was copied from it;
- that similar ideas are legally infringing;
- that a scientific claim is correct;
- that the artifact qualifies for patent protection;
- that all rights in every dependency or included file belong to Cory.

Those conclusions require their own evidence and legal/scientific analysis.
